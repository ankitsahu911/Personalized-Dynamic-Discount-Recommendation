## 1. The Project in 5 Sentences

1. This project formulates promotional retail discount recommendation across five discrete actions ($\{0\%, 5\%, 10\%, 15\%, 20\%\}$) as a contextual multi-armed bandit problem that balances volume acquisition against profit margin preservation.
2. We implement Linear Upper Confidence Bound (LinUCB) equipped with $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ Sherman-Morrison rank-1 updates alongside Bayesian contextual Thompson Sampling.
3. Recommendations are bounded by an inviolable commercial safety envelope: customer loyalty tier discount ceilings and a strict unit cost margin floor, eliminating below-cost sales by construction.
4. Evaluated over 10 independent random seeds across 20,000 online rounds and 5,000 frozen held-out rounds, the heuristic benchmark **Fixed 10% earned the highest total profit** (₹80,265.41 ± 1,788.01), whereas **LinUCB generated the highest gross revenue** (₹234,276.84 ± 4,476.98) and outperformed the commercial Rule-based baseline in 8 of 10 seeds (+₹492.50 ± 1,022.62 profit lift).
5. The fundamental scientific caveat is that all behavioral outcomes and financial figures were evaluated **within a calibrated simulation environment** based on an aggregated public retail dataset lacking individual customer identifiers, with an assumed 40% margin structure.

## 3. Core Terminology Definitions (1 Sentence Each)

- **Contextual Bandit:** A reinforcement learning algorithm that observes a multidimensional feature vector $x_t$, selects an action $a_t$, and receives immediate payoff without modeling sequential state transitions.
- **Arm:** One discrete promotional discount choice from the action set $\mathcal{A} = \{0\%, 5\%, 10\%, 15\%, 20\%\}$.
- **LinUCB:** A linear contextual bandit algorithm that selects the arm maximizing an optimistic upper confidence bound: expected payoff plus an exploration uncertainty bonus.
- **Thompson Sampling:** A Bayesian randomized decision algorithm that draws parameter vectors from a posterior distribution and selects the action that maximizes sampled return.
- **Regret:** The expected profit gap between the best possible eligible action (the Oracle) and the policy's chosen action: $\mu_t^* - \mu_t(a_t)$.
- **Oracle:** The omniscient counterfactual arm within the eligible set that maximizes true mathematical expected profit under simulator ground-truth probabilities.
- **Financial Ledger:** An immutable microeconomic accounting record tracking per-round revenue, profit, unit cost, and discount conceded with double-entry balance checks.
- **Guardrail:** A deterministic commercial rule enforced prior to model scoring that restricts allowable actions to an operational safety subset.
- **Unit Margin Floor:** An inviolable commercial threshold guaranteeing that discounted price never drops below wholesale product cost plus a buffer.
- **Online vs. Frozen Evaluation:** Online evaluation measures in-flight learning and exploration costs over 20,000 rounds; Frozen evaluation tests post-training operational policy quality on 5,000 held-out rounds.
- **Off-Policy Replay:** An offline evaluation technique using rejection sampling to evaluate candidate policies on historical logged observational transactions (Li et al. [2]).
- **Simulated Rupees (₹):** Simulated monetary units generated within the retail simulation environment.

## 4. The Three Core Topics (Claim | Evidence | Caveat)

### Topic 1: Why the Guardrail Exists
- **Claim:** Business guardrails are necessary because an unconstrained profit-maximizing bandit would learn predatory or below-cost markdowns, and soft reward penalties fail to prevent policy collapse.
- **Evidence:** Programmatic filtering (`get_eligible_actions`) achieved **100% compliance** across 200,000 online rounds (`multiseed_online_by_tier.csv`). In contrast, 39.04% of raw historical records violated tier limits (`fairness_audit.json`), and reward penalty ablation ($\lambda = 0.73$) destroyed revenue by ₹20k (`reward_ablation.csv`).
- **Caveat:** Guardrail caps restrict the bandit from testing higher discounts on New customers, slightly lowering acquisition conversion. In the app, loosening rules requires extrapolation outside the training envelope.

### Topic 2: What the Regret and Ledger Charts Prove (and What They Do Not)
- **Claim:** Regret curves prove algorithmic learning and sublinear convergence; the financial ledger demonstrates that LinUCB recovered its early exploration cost to beat commercial rule-based baselines, but lost to Fixed 10% on profit while winning on gross revenue.
- **Evidence:** Instantaneous regret flattened by over 58% from ₹0.229 to ₹0.092/round (`regret_checkpoints.csv`). The ledger proves LinUCB crossed break-even against Rule-based at Round 3,200, finishing +₹492.50 ahead, while generating highest revenue (₹234,276.84 vs. ₹232,094.81 for Fixed 10%) by offering 8.45% average discount (`comparison_online.csv`).
- **Caveat:** Regret flattens to a non-zero slope ($pprox ₹0.09/	ext{round}$) because linear models cannot fit non-linear sigmoidal demand. Both regret and ledger metrics are evaluated strictly within the simulator.

### Topic 3: Dataset and Simulation Limitations
- **Claim:** The project's findings reflect a calibrated semi-synthetic environment rather than live physical retail validation.
- **Evidence:** Public Kaggle data has 73,100 store/product/day rows with zero customer IDs. The 8,000-customer registry, baskets, and conversion outcomes are simulated by design (`registry.py`). Demand is calibrated via logistic regression with a modest ROC-AUC of 0.6322 (`evaluation_findings.md`), and unit cost assumes a 40% margin. Replay 95% confidence intervals overlap across all policies (`replay_results.csv`).
- **Caveat:** Replay serves as a sanity check, not a causal proof. In physical deployment, retailers face global promotional spend budgets and competitor price retaliation.

## 6. Known Weaknesses & Honest Viva Defenses

1. **Weakness: "Why did your bandit lose to Fixed 10% on profit?"**  
   *Defense:* "In our calibrated simulator, price elasticity has a steep margin penalty beyond 10%. Fixed 10% hit the exact sweet spot. LinUCB explored 15% and 20% markdowns to stimulate conversions—successfully capturing highest gross revenue (₹234k)—but the extra unit sales were insufficient to offset the halved unit margin. This reveals the critical commercial lesson that bandits optimize strictly what they are measured on."
2. **Weakness: "Why does LinUCB regret not flatten to zero?"**  
   *Defense:* "In theory, linear bandit regret flattens asymptotically. In our environment, demand follows a non-linear logistic sigmoid link. Because a linear model cannot fit a sigmoidal response surface across 20 dimensions, an irreducible functional approximation gap persists, resulting in a low steady residual slope."
3. **Weakness: "Your public retail dataset has no customer IDs. Isn't your personalization artificial?"**  
   *Defense:* "Yes, that is a documented limitation. The Kaggle dataset provides store/product/day logs, but individual shopper journeys were simulated via an 8,000-customer registry. Our contribution was demonstrating how contextual bandits integrate multivariate signals and guardrails; validating this on real longitudinal datasets like UCI Online Retail is our primary future work."

## 7. As-Built Design Decisions & Technical Rationales

- **Synchronized Per-Round Seeds:** Pre-generated 20,000-round sequences guarantee all 5 competing policies face identical customer arrivals, isolating algorithmic performance from environmental noise.
- **Dual Online & Frozen Modes:** Distinguishes the short-term exploration tax (Online, 20k rounds) from mature operational steady-state quality (Frozen, 5k held-out rounds).
- **Separate Tuning Seeds (101–105):** Alpha sensitivity and reward ablation were tuned on separate seeds, preventing hyperparameter over-fitting on reported evaluation seeds (42–51).
- **Sherman-Morrison Updates:** Updates inverse covariance directly in $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ instead of $\mathcal{O}(d^3)$, enabling sub-millisecond inference and fast multi-seed training on standard CPUs.
- **Empty-Set Fallback:** If margin floors and tier rules disqualify all promotional discounts, the system safely falls back to $\{0\%\}$ full retail price.
